About

My interest in communication developed before I had a name for it. Growing up, I found myself paying attention to the gap between what was being said and what was being understood — in classrooms, in news coverage, in the distance between an organisation's intentions and its audience's experience of them. Studying journalism and then advertising gave me frameworks for thinking about that gap. The MBA gave me the quantitative tools to measure it.

The work I find most engaging sits at the intersection of research and communication. Not research for its own sake, and not communication without evidence — but the particular kind of thinking that begins with a genuine question about an audience, works through what the data actually says, and ends with something a person or organisation can act upon. At Vedanta, that looked like processing customer satisfaction surveys through Excel VBA and translating the outputs into targeted messaging. At UnM Foundation, it looked like going into the field, conducting interviews, and building content from what people said rather than what we assumed they thought. The methods differ; the orientation is the same.

Outside of professional work, I completed the Bharatanatyam Visharad examination under Akhil Bhartiya Gandharva Mahavidhyalaya Mandal in 2024. The Visharad is the advanced examination in classical Indian dance — reaching it requires years of technically precise, disciplined practice under formal assessment. That process — of building competence slowly and correctly, in a domain with clear standards — has shaped how I approach most other work. Alongside that, I read broadly: psychology, communication theory, cultural studies, and occasionally fiction that is precise about how people think and why.

Areas of Work

Eight territories where interests, training, and experience converge.

01

Research & Audience Insight

The work of understanding what an audience thinks, why, and what that means for how communication should be designed. This involves survey methodology, qualitative interviews, audience segmentation, and secondary research.

I return to this kind of work because it requires asking questions before forming conclusions — which is a discipline most communication processes do not naturally encourage.

Evidence Field interviews at UnM Foundation; survey analysis at Vedanta; academic research on audience cognition and digital identity.
02

Corporate Communication

Managing how an organisation communicates with its stakeholders — internally and externally — across campaigns, events, and sustained messaging. The challenge is maintaining coherence between what an organisation intends and what its audiences receive.

This requires holding both the strategic frame and the operational detail at the same time, which suits how I tend to work.

Evidence Corporate Communication internship at Vedanta Limited — content, data analysis, event coordination, and stakeholder messaging.
03

Content Strategy

Deciding what to say, in what form, for which audience, and toward what end. The strategy here is the thinking that precedes any piece of content — the objective, the audience's current state, the desired outcome, and the gap between them.

Content without a clear reason for existing tends not to work. I find the reasoning stage more interesting than the production stage, and it shows in the quality of what gets made.

Evidence Content strategies at Brandingwaale Webtech; Environment Day campaign at Vedanta; UGC strategy at Honeywell.
04

Marketing Communication

Connecting what an organisation offers to an audience that may need it — across digital channels, paid formats, and organic content. The work involves understanding where the audience is, what they are paying attention to, and what format earns that attention.

My interest here is in the measurable side: what the data says about what works and why, rather than intuition or trend-following.

Evidence Digital marketing at Careerbuddyonline; Meta ad content at Honeywell; social media communication at UnM Foundation.
05

Brand Communication

The longer work of how an organisation is understood over time — its tone, its consistency, the impression that accumulates across every communication. Brand communication is less about individual messages and more about whether those messages add up to something coherent.

I studied this formally through advertising and public relations, and have applied it across both commercial and non-profit contexts.

Evidence BA specialisation in Advertising and Public Relations; brand strategy at Brandingwaale Webtech; product communication at Honeywell.
06

Analytics & Data Interpretation

Reading data — survey responses, post-performance metrics, segmentation outputs — and translating what it says into decisions a team can act on. This is less about the tools and more about understanding what a number actually means in context.

The most interesting part of analytics, to me, is the translation step: moving from a pattern in the data to a recommendation someone can use.

Evidence Excel VBA analysis at Vedanta; post-performance data interpretation; audience segmentation. MBA major in Marketing and Media Analytics.
07

Visual Communication

Creating visual assets that carry a message accurately and clearly. Working in Canva, Photoshop, and Illustrator — with the understanding that design decisions are communication decisions, not aesthetic ones.

My approach to visual work is informed by the same logic as written communication: understand the audience and the objective before deciding what the output should look like.

Evidence Freelance graphic design (2023); campaign visuals at Vedanta and UnM Foundation; UGC content at Honeywell. View archive ↗

Social Media Communication

Understanding social platforms as distinct communication environments — each with different audience behaviour, format constraints, and strategic logic. Producing content that is built for its platform rather than adapted to it.

My academic research on digital self-presentation on Instagram informed a more analytical understanding of how audiences behave on social platforms.

Featured Experience

Five engagements presented as case studies — context, approach, execution, and what each one clarified.

01

Vedanta Limited

Corporate Communication Intern

Chittradurga, Karnataka

April – June 2025

Context

Vedanta Limited is a large natural resources corporation operating across multiple business verticals. The communication function manages a significant volume of internal and external messaging, with formal reporting structures, large workforce audiences, and access to substantial organisational data collected through ongoing survey programmes.

Approach

The internship ran across two parallel workstreams: an analytical workstream involving survey data, and a communication workstream involving content and event execution. Rather than treating these as separate tasks, the approach was to let the data inform the communication decisions wherever possible — using what the survey results said to understand audience perception before deciding what the messaging should do.

Work Done

Used Excel VBA to process FY 2024–25 customer satisfaction survey data, segmenting responses by relevant variables to identify patterns in audience perception and generate inputs for targeted messaging strategy. Analysed post-performance data across communication channels to assess which content approaches were generating meaningful engagement. Created content for Vedanta's Environment Day campaign — developing messaging from brief through to final assets. Planned and coordinated an internal staff engagement event, managing logistics, communication, and execution.

What This Demonstrated

That communication decisions made without data and data collected without a communication purpose are both incomplete. The most useful work at Vedanta happened at the intersection of the two — when the survey findings shaped the content approach, rather than running alongside it. Understanding how an audience segment actually feels before writing for them produces different work than assuming you already know.

02

Honeywell

Content Marketing Intern

Secure Connections

July – September 2025

Context

Honeywell's Secure Connections vertical required product content for deployment in paid digital advertising on Meta platforms. The content brief was performance-oriented — the output needed to function within the specific constraints of the ad format and contribute to measurable commercial outcomes, not simply represent the product accurately.

Approach

UGC-style content for paid advertising requires a different frame than campaign or editorial content. The approach was to understand the audience's decision process first — what they are evaluating, what objections they hold, and what kind of peer validation is credible — before deciding how to structure the content. Format, register, and platform context all affect whether paid content converts, independent of product quality.

Work Done

Developed UGC-format content for Honeywell's audio product range, scripted and produced for use in Meta advertising campaigns. The content was deployed directly in paid campaigns during the internship period and contributed to an increase in sales for the product line.

What This Demonstrated

Content created for performance environments is a different discipline from content created for awareness or engagement. It requires understanding the audience's decision journey, not just their awareness of a product. The gap between content that is accurate and content that converts is almost always a gap in audience understanding — knowing what someone needs to believe before they act, and building the content around that specific need.

03

UnM Foundation

Marketing Intern

Pune, Maharashtra

November 2024

Context

UnM Foundation is a Pune-based non-profit organisation working on road safety awareness. The communication challenge was reaching a broad public audience with a message requiring both emotional engagement and genuine behavioural intent — a harder combination to achieve than either alone. The foundation required both content production for social media and insight generation to understand how existing awareness was landing with the public.

Approach

The work divided naturally into two modes: a production mode (creating content for existing platforms and audiences) and a research mode (going into the field to understand how those audiences were actually thinking about road safety). The second mode informed the first, and the presence of primary field data made the content decisions more defensible.

Work Done

Created a range of content formats — reels and static posts — to promote the Foundation's initiatives and build online engagement around its road safety programme. Conducted field interviews with members of the public to gather primary insight on how road safety was perceived and how existing campaigns were being received. Interview findings fed back into content decisions made during the engagement.

What This Demonstrated

Field research produces different information than analytics. What a person says in a direct conversation — their hesitations, their specific knowledge gaps, their actual language — is not visible in engagement metrics or platform data. Both sources have limitations and blind spots; the most accurate picture of an audience comes from treating them as complementary rather than equivalent. That understanding has changed how I approach content decisions.

Brandingwaale Webtech

Marketing Intern

Digital Marketing Agency

September – November 2024

Context

A digital marketing agency working across multiple client brands simultaneously. The agency context requires maintaining distinct voice, positioning, and strategic logic for each client while operating within the same team and workflow — a discipline that develops systematic thinking about brand differentiation.

Approach

Each client brand required its own research base before strategy or content work began. The approach was to treat every engagement as a new audience question: who are they speaking to, what does that audience already know or believe, and what does the strategy need to change or reinforce.

Work Done

Conducted market research to map competitive positioning for client brands. Developed content strategies and produced social media content across multiple client accounts. Contributed to business development conversations, including research and prospecting support.

What This Demonstrated

Working across multiple client contexts simultaneously requires a systematic approach to each one — it is not possible to carry assumptions from one brand into another. The agency environment made visible something that is easy to miss in single-client work: that every strategy is only as good as the quality of the audience understanding it begins with. When that understanding is shallow, the content strategy that follows it reflects that.

Context

Careerbuddyonline is a career development platform in an early growth stage. The digital marketing work here was foundational — building discoverability through search-optimised content and digital advertising at a point when the organisation was still establishing its audience base.

Approach

SEO-based content strategy requires understanding what an audience is already looking for before deciding what the organisation should say. The work began with search intent research rather than with the product's own framing — a discipline that changes how content is structured, not just how it is written.

Work Done

The relationship between search behaviour and content strategy is not obvious until you have worked inside it. What an audience is actively looking for is often different from what an organisation thinks it should be offering. Effective SEO content requires understanding that gap — not closing it by changing the message, but by finding the accurate intersection between what the organisation does and what the audience is already seeking. This was the first context where I understood audience behaviour as something to read before something to write for.

Research

Academic inquiry and primary research as professional practice, not student exercise.

Interests & Orientation

My academic and professional work has consistently returned to a set of related questions: how audiences form impressions, how digital environments shape self-presentation and identity, how communication affects perception and behaviour, and what survey and interview data can reveal that surface-level metrics cannot.

These are not narrow interests. They are orientations — toward understanding people before designing messages for them. The research question, when taken seriously, changes the communication output.

Approach

My approach to research is mixed-methods by preference and structured by habit. I am comfortable with quantitative instruments — survey design, segmentation, Excel-based data processing including VBA — and with qualitative methods, including semi-structured interviews and field-based insight gathering.

I treat data as the beginning of interpretation, not the end of it. The pattern in the data is only useful when there is a question it was designed to answer.

Dissertation research is currently being developed toward a journal publication. Further details available on request.

Research Projects
Academic Research · Survey-Based

You Are Smarter Than You Think

An inquiry into cognitive self-assessment and its relationship to communication behaviour. The study examined how individuals perceive their own intellectual capability relative to others, and how this perception influences their engagement with information and messaging. The research employed survey methodology with analysis oriented toward identifying patterns across demographic variables. The findings have implications for how communications practitioners design messages intended to reach audiences who may underestimate their own capacity to engage with complex information.

Academic Research · Digital Communication

Who Am I in the World of Instagram

A study of digital self-presentation and identity construction on Instagram. The research examined how users negotiate personal identity against platform norms, audience expectations, and visibility pressures. Grounded in communication theory and digital culture studies, the project explored the gap between expressed and experienced identity in social media environments. The study contributes to a growing body of work on how platform architecture shapes communicative behaviour — with practical implications for how organisations understand and communicate with social media audiences.
